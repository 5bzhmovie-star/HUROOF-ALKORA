L166: [cite94†edit ]
L167: 
L168: In 2024, SAFF (Saudi Arabian Football Federation) revealed a newly redesigned trophy of the Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques Cup during a ceremony held in Riyadh. A new trophy was required because under the competition's regulations, a team that wins the cup three consecutive times or four times in total has the right to keep the original trophy.^{cite95†[4] }
L169: Made by British silversmiths, cite96†Thomas Lyte , the trophy is made of sterling silver coated with 24-carat gold plate. It weighs 9.32 kg, and is 54 cm high, 18 cm wide, has a base diameter of 17 cm and is made of marble and malachite.^{cite97†[5] }

L140: ## History
L141: 
L142: [cite76†edit ]
L143: The idea of having a super cup tournament in Saudi Arabia dates back to 1979 when a cite77†two-legged match was held between the cite78†1978–79 Saudi Premier League winners, cite65†Al-Hilal , and the cite79†1979 King Cup winners, cite64†Al-Ahli .
L144: The first leg, which was played in Jeddah, ended in 2–2 draw and the second leg, held in Riyadh, ended in a 4–1 win for Al-Hilal.^{cite80†[1] } 20 years later, another super cup match between Al-Hilal, the cite81†1997–98 Saudi Premier League winners, and Al-Ahli, the 1997–98 Saudi Crown Prince Cup winners, was held. It was the opening match for the inaugural cite82†Saudi Founder's Cup and ended in a 5–2 win for Al-Hilal.^{cite83†[2] }
L145: In 2012, cite62†Saudi Arabian Football Federation officially decided to launch the tournament following the conclusion of the 2011–12 season.
L146: The planned super cup match was set to be held between the cite84†2011–12 Saudi Pro League winners, cite85†Al-Shabab and the cite86†2012 King Cup of Champions winners, Al-Ahli.^{cite87†[3] } However, the 2012 Super Cup was canceled due to scheduling issues as no appropriate date for the match was found.^{cite88†[4] } The tournament was officially inaugurated in 2013 and was played by the cite89†2012–13 Saudi Pro League winners, cite90†Al-Fateh , and cite91†2013 King Cup of Champions winners, cite92†Al-Ittihad .^{cite93†[5] } Al-Fateh won the first official edition of the Saudi Super Cup after beating Al-Ittihad 3–2 after extra time.^{cite94†[6] } The cite95†2014 Saudi Super Cup was the first edition to be held in cite96†Riyadh .
L147: cite85†Al-Shabab defeated cite97†Al-Nassr 4–3 on penalties in the cite98†King Fahd International Stadium .^{cite99†[7] } In 2015, the Super Cup was played outside of Saudi Arabia for the first time. The match was between cite100†2014–15 Saudi Pro League winners, cite97†Al-Nassr , and cite101†2015 King Cup winners, Al-Hilal.
L148: The match was held in cite102†Loftus Road in cite103†London and ended in 1–0 win for Al-Hilal.^{cite104†[8] } The next two editions were also held in London, with the 2016 edition held in cite105†Craven Cottage and the 2018 edition held once again in Loftus Road. In 2016, cite64†Al-Ahli defeated Al-Hilal 4–3 on penalties.^{cite106†[9] } The 2017 edition which was supposed to be contested between Al-Hilal and Al-Ittihad was the first edition to be canceled.
L149: The decision was based on the request of then-Saudi national team manager, cite107†Edgardo Bauza , who expressed his desire to change the calendar of the season to help him set the ideal preparation program for the cite108†2018 FIFA World Cup .^{cite109†[10] } In 2018, the tournament returned following a years absence. Al-Hilal defeated Al-Ittihad 2–1 to become the first team to win the trophy twice.^{cite110†[11] } In cite111†2019 , the Saudi Super Cup was held in cite112†Jeddah for the first time.
